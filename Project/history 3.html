The modern history of India is a period that begins with the decline of the Mughal Empire and spans the colonial rule by the British East India Company and the subsequent struggle for independence. Here are some key events and developments in modern Indian history:

British Colonial Rule (1757-1947):

The British East India Company established control over various regions in India, starting with the Battle of Plassey in 1757 and the Battle of Buxar in 1764.
The British gradually expanded their influence and established a formal colonial administration.
India became a vital part of the British Empire, with significant economic exploitation and cultural impact.
The 1857 Indian Rebellion, also known as the Sepoy Mutiny or the First War of Independence, marked a major turning point in the struggle for independence. However, it was ultimately suppressed by the British.
Formation of Indian National Congress (1885):

The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded as a political party dedicated to achieving self-governance within the British Empire. It played a crucial role in the struggle for independence.
Partition of Bengal (1905):

The British decision to partition the province of Bengal for administrative reasons led to widespread protests and opposition, eventually resulting in the province's reunification in 1911.
World War I (1914-1918):

India's participation in World War I, coupled with the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms, raised hopes of political reforms and eventual self-rule.
Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919):

The massacre of hundreds of unarmed Indian civilians by British troops in Amritsar led to widespread outrage and marked a turning point in the struggle for independence.
Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience Movements:

Under the leadership of Mahatma Gandhi, Indians launched non-violent movements to resist British rule. These included the Non-Cooperation Movement and the Civil Disobedience Movement, which aimed at achieving political and economic independence.
World War II (1939-1945):

India's contribution to the British war effort during World War II, combined with the Quit India Movement of 1942, increased pressure on the British to address Indian demands for independence.
Independence and Partition (1947):

India gained independence on August 15, 1947, and was partitioned into two separate nations, India and Pakistan, based on religious lines. This event led to significant communal violence and mass migrations.
Constitution of India (1950):

India adopted its own constitution, establishing a democratic republic. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar played a pivotal role in drafting the Indian Constitution.
Nehruvian Era (1947-1964):

Jawaharlal Nehru became India's first Prime Minister and laid the foundation for modern India's democratic and socialist principles.
Sino-Indian War (1962) and Indo-Pak Wars (1947-48, 1965, and 1971):

India was involved in conflicts with both China and Pakistan, with the most significant victory being the 1971 war, leading to the creation of Bangladesh.
Economic Reforms (1991):

India initiated a series of economic liberalization measures in 1991, opening up its economy to globalization and foreign investment.
Political Developments (Post-Independence):

India has seen various political shifts, including the dominance of the Congress Party, the rise of regional parties, and the ascent of the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) to power.
